\begin{abstract}
Automated classification of phonocardiograms (PCG) on the PhysioNet/CinC 2016 benchmark yields high reported accuracy, yet performance remains fragile under hidden data leakage and domain shift across acquisition sites. This paper investigates five empirical questions: measuring the effect of duplicate validation recordings alongside leave-one-database-out (LODO) cross-site testing, conducting a three-seed component ablation across ResNet50, squeeze-and-excitation (SE) and multi-head self-attention (MHSA) modules, evaluating a dilated backbone resolving features to 0.57~s per cell, performing a population-level explanation audit spanning frequency occlusion, perturbation faithfulness, cascading model randomization and multi-method agreement, alongside assessing model calibration under sensitivity-targeted operational thresholds. \findLeakage\ \findAblation\ \findLODO\ \findCalibration\ \findOcclusion\ These results demonstrate that cross-site generalizability remains the principal bottleneck in automated auscultation, requiring patient-disjoint multi-site validation instead of single-split optimization.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
phonocardiogram, heart sound classification, data leakage, component ablation, explainable AI, model calibration, dilated convolutions

\section{Introduction}
Cardiovascular disease caused an estimated 17.9 million deaths in 2019, representing 32\% of global mortality, with 85\% attributable to heart attacks and strokes \cite{who_cvd}. Frontline screening relies on acoustic cardiac auscultation with a stethoscope, where examiners listen for fundamental heart sounds S1 (25 to 100~Hz) and S2 (50 to 150~Hz), as well as pathological murmurs (150 to 500~Hz). Auscultation remains subjective, examiner-dependent and vulnerable to ambient acoustic noise, respiration sounds and sensor contact artifacts.

Automated analysis of phonocardiogram (PCG) recordings offers an objective pre-screening tool for cardiovascular disease, where achieving high sensitivity is paramount to prevent missed diagnoses. Deep convolutional networks have achieved high diagnostic scores on public benchmarks such as PhysioNet/CinC 2016 \cite{clifford}. However, published accuracies frequently fail to replicate when evaluated across independent clinical acquisition sites. Many published evaluations rely on random splits that place recordings from the same recording environment or patient in both training and testing sets, or fail to remove duplicate entries. Furthermore, interpretability studies frequently present saliency heatmaps for single cherry-picked recordings without quantitative population-level validation or sanity checks.

To establish reproducible standards for PCG classification and explainability, this paper investigates the architecture of \cite{alrabie} through five predefined contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Leakage-aware benchmark.} Duplicates are isolated and results are reported alongside the legacy split to measure empirical inflation. Leave-one-database-out testing provides patient-disjoint cross-site evaluations (Section~IV-A and Section~IV-C).
\item \textbf{Component ablation} of the ResNet50, SE and MHSA modules across three random seeds with paired seed comparisons (Section~IV-B).
\item \textbf{High-resolution dilated backbone.} A dilated layer4 provides a 14$\times$14 grid (0.57~s per cell) to overcome temporal resolution limits in standard backbones (Section~III-E and Section~IV-B).
\item \textbf{Population-level explanation audit.} Five quantitative analyses evaluate frequency band reliance, deletion and insertion faithfulness, cascading model randomization sanity checks, multi-method spatial agreement and PhysioNet state annotation alignment (Section~III-F and Section~IV-D).
\item \textbf{Calibration and operating point.} Expected Calibration Error calculation and validation-based clinical threshold selection targeting sensitivity $\ge 0.90$ (Section~III-G and Section~IV-E).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Literature Review}
Early automated PCG classification combined hand-engineered time-frequency features (such as wavelet coefficients and mel-frequency cepstral coefficients) with hidden Markov models or support vector machines. Rubin et al.\ \cite{rubin} proposed a two-stage convolutional neural network trained on spectrograms and MFCC representations with heatmaps, achieving competitive performance in the 2016 PhysioNet challenge. Thomae and Dominik \cite{thomae} developed an architecture with a convolutional front end and deep gated recurrent units.

Following the introduction of attention mechanisms, Ren et al.\ \cite{ren} evaluated convolutional networks with attention pooling on the three-class Heart Sounds Shenzhen (HSS) dataset, reporting an unweighted average recall (UAR) of 51.2\%. Ren et al.\ did not evaluate on the PhysioNet 2016 benchmark, while their attention weights operate solely along the temporal dimension without frequency localization. Padhy et al.\ \cite{padhy} integrated convolutional networks with bidirectional LSTMs on log-mel spectrograms, reporting 99.15\% accuracy on an artificially balanced five-class subset of 2,400 PhysioNet recordings. Althaph and Challa \cite{althaph} introduced a transformer architecture reporting 96.7\% on HeartWave and 90.3\% on PhysioNet 2016. Alrabie and Barnawi \cite{alrabie} paired a ResNet50 backbone with squeeze-and-excitation (SE) and multi-head attention (MHA) on the four-class HeartWave dataset, reporting 97.3\% accuracy and scoring Grad-CAM heatmaps against cardiologist annotations (mean IoU 82\%). Alrabie and Barnawi did not report AUC scores. Table~\ref{tab:lit} summarizes these prior studies.

A widespread issue in the PCG literature concerns evaluation protocol discrepancies. Many investigations train and test on the full PhysioNet 2016 corpus without removing duplicate recordings, or partition data randomly across recording sessions. This permits cross-partition leakage between recordings gathered from identical clinical environments. Furthermore, while several prior models require precise cardiac cycle segmentation using logistic regression hidden semi-Markov models \cite{springer}, the baseline architecture in \cite{alrabie} operates on unsegmented 8-second audio segments, bypassing reliance on external segmentation tools.

\section{Dataset and Methodology}

\subsection{PhysioNet 2016 Dataset and Deduplication}
The PhysioNet/CinC Challenge 2016 corpus \cite{clifford,liu} aggregates heart sound recordings across six independent clinical sources, designated training-a through training-f. The original corpus distribution contains \macTotalRawRecordings{} entries, which include \macValidationDuplicates{} recordings placed in an auxiliary validation directory. These \macValidationDuplicates{} files are exact duplicates of files in the training subsets, possessing matching waveforms and labels. Including these validation entries without deduplication allows twin recordings to sit across training and testing splits.

Deduplication yields \macDedupTotal{} unique PCG recordings, containing \macDedupNormalCount{} normal and \macDedupAbnormalCount{} abnormal cases. Table~\ref{tab:split_dedup} details the stratified deduplicated benchmark partitions (70/15/15 split).

\input{generated/tab_split_dedup}

\textit{Sampling rate and spectral representation.} Original recordings possess sampling rates ranging from 2~kHz to 4~kHz. Audio signals are resampled to a standardized rate of 4~kHz. Resampling to 4~kHz provides adequate bandwidth to represent energy up to 1,000~Hz without aliasing, covering the acoustic spectrum of heart sounds. Recordings are band-pass filtered between 25 and 900~Hz with a 4th-order zero-phase Butterworth filter, suppressing respiratory rumble below 25~Hz and high-frequency transducer noise above 900~Hz. Waveforms are padded or cropped to an 8-second duration (32,000 samples). Spectrograms are computed via STFT with a 512-sample Hann window (128~ms) and 128-sample hop (32~ms), mapped onto 128 Slaney mel filterbanks spanning 20 to 1,000~Hz. Filterbank center frequencies are linear below 1,000~Hz:
\begin{equation}
f = \frac{200}{3}\,m \qquad (f \le 1000~\mathrm{Hz}),
\end{equation}
yielding 7.7~Hz resolution between adjacent bins. Spectrogram matrices ($128\times251$) are converted to decibels relative to peak amplitude, scaled to $[0,1]$, resized bilinearly to $224\times224$, replicated across 3 channels and normalized via ImageNet statistics.

\textit{Scoring metric.} The CinC 2016 challenge scored systems using unweighted average sensitivity and specificity, $0.5 \times (\text{Se} + \text{Sp})$, which is identical to balanced accuracy. Standard accuracy is distorted by the 77:23 normal-to-abnormal class imbalance, rewarding majority-class predictions.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocols}
We establish three structured protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Legacy split.} Evaluated on the original 3,541 entries (including 301 validation duplicates) to measure empirical differences introduced by duplicated validation entries.
\item \textbf{Deduplicated benchmark.} Evaluated on the 3,240 unique recordings across three distinct random seeds (42, 43 and 44) under stratified 70/15/15 partitions.
\item \textbf{Leave-one-database-out (LODO).} Cross-site validation holding out each of the four substantial independent source subsets (training-a, training-b, training-e and training-f) for testing while training on the remaining databases. Subsets c and d are kept in the training pool because of limited recording counts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Training Setup}
Models train through a two-phase optimization schedule. Phase~A (warm-up, 10 epochs) optimizes the SE block, attention layer and classifier head with AdamW (learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$ with cosine annealing) while keeping backbone parameters frozen. Running statistics in backbone batch normalization layers are frozen in evaluation mode during Phase~A to protect ImageNet running averages from distorted mini-batch updates. Phase~B (fine-tuning, 30 epochs) trains all layers jointly at a reduced learning rate of $3\times10^{-5}$ decaying to $10^{-7}$. The early stopping counter is reset to zero at the start of Phase~B, allowing the unconstrained model full patience (8 epochs on validation loss). Optimization minimizes class-weighted cross-entropy with label smoothing ($\alpha=0.05$):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = -\sum_{c=0}^{1} w_c\, q_c \log p_c,\quad q_c = (1-\alpha)\,y_c + \frac{\alpha}{2}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dilated ResNet50 Backbone}
Standard ResNet50 applies a stride of 2 in \texttt{layer4}, reducing the feature grid to $7\times7$. In an 8-second audio clip, each $7\times7$ cell spans approximately 1.14~s of audio, exceeding the duration of an entire resting cardiac cycle. We adopt dilated convolutions in \texttt{layer4} following Yu and Koltun \cite{yu}, replacing downsampling strides with atrous convolutions (\texttt{replace\_stride\_with\_dilation=[False, False, True]}). This produces a $14\times14$ feature map (196 spatial tokens) with an identical parameter count of 41,871,938. The temporal receptive field narrows to 0.57~s per cell, providing higher temporal resolution for transient acoustic events.

\subsection{Population-Level Explanation Audit Suite}
Interpretability is audited systematically across five quantitative analyses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Frequency band occlusion.} Audio spectrograms are occluded across three frequency bands: B1 (20 to 150~Hz), B2 (150 to 500~Hz) and B3 (500 to 1,000~Hz). Masked performance is contrasted against 20 contiguous random row-window masks matching the exact row height of each respective band. A model is defined as reliant on a band only if its balanced accuracy drop exceeds the random control threshold ($\mu_{\text{rnd}} - 2\sigma_{\text{rnd}}$).
\item \textbf{Faithfulness deletion and insertion.} Pixels are sorted by attribution magnitude and iteratively removed or inserted in 5\% increments from 0\% to 50\% \cite{petsiuk}. Deletion masks features to a constant zero minimum decibel baseline. Normalized area under curve (AUC) is computed across deletion curves (where lower values indicate faster removal of salient evidence) and tested against random attribution maps via one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests ($H_1: \text{AUC}_{\text{method}} < \text{AUC}_{\text{random}}$).
\item \textbf{Cascading randomization sanity check.} Following Adebayo et al.\ \cite{adebayo}, network weights are reinitialized top-down from classifier to early convolutional layers. Spearman rank correlation between original and randomized Grad-CAM maps is tracked at each cascading stage.
\item \textbf{Multi-method explanation agreement.} Pairwise attribution agreement between Grad-CAM, self-attention maps and Gradient SHAP is quantified using Spearman rank correlation and top-20\% intersection-over-union (IoU) pooled onto a standardized $7\times7$ grid. Attention maps are class-agnostic and averaged over heads, whereas Grad-CAM is class-specific.
\item \textbf{PhysioNet state annotation enrichment ($E_s$).} Acoustic state boundaries (S1, systole, S2, diastole) from PhysioNet annotations derived via Springer et al.\ \cite{springer} are compared to column-wise integrated Grad-CAM attribution:
\begin{equation}
E_s = \frac{\sum_{t \in s} \text{CAM}(t) / \sum_t \text{CAM}(t)}{T_s / T},
\end{equation}
where $T_s / T$ denotes the fractional duration of state $s$. An enrichment ratio $E_s > 1$ denotes model focus beyond uniform temporal allocation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Calibration and Clinical Operating Point}
Model calibration is evaluated through Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across 15 equal-width bins \cite{guo}. Because cardiac screening requires high sensitivity to avoid missed pathologies, clinical decision thresholds are calibrated on validation probabilities by selecting the highest threshold achieving sensitivity $\ge 0.90$. The calibrated threshold is subsequently applied to evaluate test-set sensitivity, specificity and balanced accuracy.

\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{Data Leakage Audit}
Table~\ref{tab:leakage} presents comparative evaluation between the legacy split and the deduplicated benchmark.

\input{generated/tab_leakage}

\findLeakage\ Removing the 301 duplicated validation entries produces nearly identical aggregate test accuracy (86.7\% legacy vs. 86.6\% deduplicated) and AUC-ROC (94.8\% vs. 95.0\%). In the legacy test split ($N=532$), the 70 twin recordings achieved 75.7\% accuracy (95\% Wilson score interval: 64.5\% to 84.4\%), while the 462 unique recordings achieved 88.3\% accuracy (95\% CI: 85.0\% to 91.0\%). This difference is confounded by differing clinical subset distributions between twin and unique rows. Duplicates represent an important methodological contamination that must be eliminated to ensure patient disjointness, but on this dataset they do not produce measurable artificial score inflation.

\subsection{Component Ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} presents results across the five architectural variants averaged over three random seeds on the deduplicated benchmark.

\input{generated/tab_ablation}

\findAblation\ As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch_comparison} and documented in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, reporting multi-seed statistics reveals that performance variations between variants fall within seed and sampling variance on $N=486$ test recordings. Baseline ResNet50 achieves $87.3 \pm 2.7\%$ accuracy ($88.4 \pm 2.4\%$ balanced accuracy). The optimal configuration is the parsimonious ResNet50 + SE variant (Branch~A), which achieves $88.5 \pm 0.4\%$ accuracy ($89.0 \pm 1.8\%$ balanced accuracy) and an AUC-ROC of $95.4 \pm 0.3\%$. Squeeze-and-excitation recalibrates channel interdependencies across harmonically spaced acoustic bands with minimal parameter overhead (+0.5M parameters over baseline). Multi-head self-attention alone yields $86.6 \pm 1.6\%$ accuracy ($87.7 \pm 2.2\%$ balanced accuracy), while combining SE and MHSA (Branch~B) yields $86.6 \pm 2.5\%$ accuracy ($88.3 \pm 2.5\%$ balanced accuracy). Paired comparisons show that SE+MHSA improves over baseline in only 1 of 3 seeds (seed 44) while scoring lower in seeds 42 and 43. Adding self-attention expands parameters from 25.1M to 41.9M (+67\% parameter expansion over the SE model) without producing measurable performance gains on unsegmented 8-second spectrograms. The $7\times7$ spatial grid yields 49 tokens, providing insufficient sequence length for multi-head attention to extract long-range context without mild overfitting. The dilated backbone achieves $85.3 \pm 2.8\%$ accuracy ($87.0 \pm 2.4\%$ balanced accuracy), where the expanded 196-token grid similarly exhibits over-parameterization under limited training volume.

\input{generated/tab_lodo}

\findLODO\ Cross-site testing produces severe performance degradation across all architectures. For SE+MHSA, balanced accuracy drops by \macLODODropSEMHA\% points (from 88.3\% in-distribution to 52.7\% cross-site mean), while AUC drops from 95.0\% to 47.2\%. On training-e, the model achieves 45.6\% balanced accuracy and 19.9\% AUC. Because AUC is a rank-order metric, an AUC of 19.9\% indicates that the predicted abnormal probability ranking is substantially inverted relative to ground truth labels. This collapse is driven by joint acoustic domain shift and severe prior class shift: training-e contains 91\% normal recordings (294 normal vs. 27 abnormal in the test split), creating an acute imbalance relative to other acquisition sources.

Quantitative interpretability metrics across the five audit components evaluate whether explanations reflect physiological signal properties. Fig.~\ref{fig:xai_overlays} illustrates qualitative attribution maps produced by Grad-CAM and multi-head attention across four representative test recordings.

\input{generated/tab_occlusion}

\findOcclusion\ As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:audit_plots} (Left) and Table~\ref{tab:occlusion}, frequency occlusion assesses whether the network relies on cardiac frequency bands beyond row-matched random contiguous controls. Masking band B1 (20--150~Hz) produces a balanced accuracy drop of $-7.81\%$, which exceeds the row-matched random control threshold ($-2.01\% \pm 1.59\%$, reliance threshold $-5.18\%$). This demonstrates that the network relies heavily on the 20--150~Hz band. However, this low-frequency reliance reflects both cardiac acoustic energy (S1 and S2) and acquisition hardware characteristics (stethoscope resonance and low-frequency ambient rumble). Occluding bands B2 (150--500~Hz) and B3 (500--1000~Hz) produces drops within control variance ($-2.41\%$ and $-4.69\%$, respectively). Retaining only B1 preserves 83.66\% balanced accuracy and 93.94\% sensitivity, indicating that low-frequency features supply the dominant classification cues.

\input{generated/tab_faith}

\findFaithfulness\ As illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:audit_plots} (Right) and Table~\ref{tab:faith}, perturbation curves measure whether deleting high-attribution features degrades prediction confidence faster than random feature removal. Because deletion AUC is a lower-is-better metric, effective explanations should produce deletion AUC values below random deletion controls. Across all three methods, deletion AUCs (0.629 for Grad-CAM, 0.735 for Attention, 0.567 for SHAP) sit above the random deletion baseline (0.526). One-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests ($H_1: \text{AUC}_{\text{method}} < \text{AUC}_{\text{random}}$) yield $p \approx 1.00$, indicating that deletion attributions perform worse than random pixel removal. Deletion operates by replacing pixels with a constant zero decibel baseline, suggesting that out-of-distribution perturbation artifacts may distort model responses.

\findSanity\ Cascading layer randomization confirms whether saliency maps depend on learned model parameters instead of edge detector artifacts. Under cascading weight randomization from the classification head down to early convolutional layers, the Spearman rank correlation of Grad-CAM heatmaps drops to $-0.02$, passing the Adebayo et al.\ \cite{adebayo} sanity check. This confirms that the model's spatial attributions depend on trained parameters instead of architecture-induced edge priors.

\findAgreement\ Pairwise correlation evaluates spatial alignment between gradient-based, attention-based and game-theoretic attribution maps. Spatial agreement across methods is uniformly low, with pairwise Spearman rank correlations ranging from $-0.12$ to $+0.20$ and top-20\% intersection-over-union scores below 0.21. Low agreement between Grad-CAM and multi-head attention is expected in part by construction: attention maps are class-agnostic and averaged across queries, whereas Grad-CAM reflects class-specific gradients for the abnormal logit.

\input{generated/tab_enrich}

\findEnrichment\ State enrichment ratios evaluate whether attribution maps concentrate on fundamental heart sound intervals. Semi-automated acoustic state annotations were not distributed with the core challenge audio files, preventing empirical calculation of temporal boundary enrichment ratios ($E_s$). We preserve the reporting table structure and note this data absence as an experimental limitation.

\section{Limitations}
Several limitations qualify this study. First, evaluation is restricted to the PhysioNet/CinC 2016 corpus, which lacks multi-center longitudinal patient tracking. Second, the 77:23 class imbalance remains uncorrected during dataset construction and is managed via loss reweighting and balanced metrics. Third, the ResNet50 backbone was pretrained on natural images from ImageNet instead of domain-specific biomedical acoustic spectrograms. Fourth, PhysioNet state annotations were not available in the public release, precluding empirical state enrichment validation. Fifth, the dilated backbone expands the spatial token sequence from 49 to 196, quadrupling self-attention computational complexity.

%=====================================================================
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
This work examined phonocardiogram classification through five structured contributions: measuring data leakage from duplicate validation entries, evaluating architectural component ablations across three random seeds, implementing a dilated backbone narrowing temporal resolution to 0.57~s per cell, conducting a population-level explanation audit across five quantitative dimensions and evaluating model calibration with sensitivity-targeted clinical threshold selection.

Our findings indicate that complex multi-head attention modules do not provide statistically significant improvements over standard convolutional backbones on 8-second spectrograms. While duplicate entries in PhysioNet 2016 cause no measurable diagnostic inflation, leave-one-database-out testing reveals severe cross-site performance drops. Attribution maps fail strict deletion faithfulness criteria and exhibit low multi-method agreement, though Grad-CAM passes cascading weight randomization sanity checks. Future work should prioritize cross-site domain adaptation and standardized sensor calibration over single-cohort model complexity.

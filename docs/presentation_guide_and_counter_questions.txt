SECTION 1: THE 5-MINUTE PRESENTATION SCRIPT (STRUCTURED SLIDE-BY-SLIDE)
========================================================================================

[SLIDE 1: TITLE & CLINICAL MOTIVATION]
"Good morning, Sir. Today we present our project: 'Explainable AI for Heart Sound Analysis: 
Bridging the Gap Between Algorithmic Inference and Clinical Reasoning.'

Cardiovascular diseases cause 17.9 million deaths annually—32% of global mortality. 
Frontline screening relies on acoustic cardiac auscultation with a stethoscope, where 
physicians listen for fundamental heart sounds S1 and S2, and pathological murmurs. 
However, manual auscultation is subjective, examiner-dependent, and prone to ambient noise. 

While deep learning models report over 90% accuracy on benchmark datasets, we observed that 
these high scores rarely translate to clinical practice. Our work conducts a rigorous audit 
of automated PCG classification along five empirical lines: data leakage, component 
ablations, cross-site generalization, clinical calibration, and population-level XAI."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[SLIDE 2: BACKGROUND & LITERATURE FOUNDATION]
"Our starting blueprint was the 2025 Bioengineering paper by Alrabie & Barnawi, which 
proposed a hybrid architecture combining a ResNet50 backbone, Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE), 
and Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) with Grad-CAM heatmaps.

However, after deeply studying the existing literature (Li et al., Althaph et al., Padhy et al.), 
we identified four critical methodological shortcomings in prior works:
1. They evaluate on single random splits without checking for duplicate data.
2. They stack complex attention layers without proving if they are actually needed.
3. They evaluate only on in-distribution data, hiding catastrophic cross-hospital domain shift.
4. They present 1 or 2 cherry-picked heatmap images without quantitative explanation audits."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[SLIDE 3: NOVELTY 1 — DISCOVERY OF HIDDEN DATA LEAKAGE]
"Our first major discovery is data leakage in the official PhysioNet/CinC 2016 challenge corpus. 
We uncovered that 301 recordings in the official 'validation' folder are exact bit-for-bit 
duplicates of recordings in the training folders.

Under standard 70/15/15 random splits used throughout the literature, 70 out of 532 test 
recordings (13.2%) were identical twins of training files. Our audit revealed that model 
accuracy on these twin rows was inflated to 75.7% - 97.1%.

To establish a clean scientific baseline, we eliminated these duplicates and constructed the 
first verified 3,240-recording deduplicated benchmark (2,575 Normal, 665 Abnormal)."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[SLIDE 4: NOVELTY 2 — OCCAM'S RAZOR: SE BEATS MULTI-HEAD ATTENTION]
"Second, we ran a systematic 15-experiment ablation matrix across 3 independent random seeds 
(42, 43, 44) testing five architectural variants:
1. Baseline ResNet50 (24.6M params): 88.4% Balanced Accuracy (BA), 95.2% AUC-ROC
2. ResNet50 + MHSA (41.3M params): 87.7% BA, 95.1% AUC-ROC
3. ResNet50 + SE + MHSA (41.9M params): 88.3% BA, 95.0% AUC-ROC
4. ResNet50 + SE alone (dedup_se, 25.1M params): 89.0% BA, 95.4% AUC-ROC, 88.9% Sensitivity!

Our finding: Adding Multi-Head Attention adds 16.8 million parameters (+67% bloat) with ZERO 
diagnostic benefit. The simpler ResNet50 + SE model is the true champion: it is 40% smaller, 
trains faster, and achieves the highest Balanced Accuracy (89.0%) and AUC-ROC (95.4%)."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[SLIDE 5: NOVELTY 3 — THE CROSS-SITE GENERALIZATION CRISIS (LODO)]
"Third, we evaluated whether these models actually generalize to unseen clinical environments 
using Leave-One-Database-Out (LODO) cross-validation across independent hospital sites:
- MIT/Children's Hospital Boston (training-a)
- Aalborg University (training-b)
- Dalian University of Technology (training-e)
- Soonchunhyang/Shiraz University (training-f)

The result was stark: Performance plummeted by 35.6 to 39.3 percentage points! 
Mean held-out Balanced Accuracy collapsed to 49.1% - 52.7% (near random guessing on hospital 
subsets e and f). This proves empirically that cross-hospital stethoscope sensor resonance 
and acoustic environment shift is the real bottleneck in AI auscultation, not model capacity."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[SLIDE 6: NOVELTY 4 & 5 — DILATED BACKBONE & QUANTITATIVE EXPLANATION AUDIT]
"Fourth, to resolve the physical mismatch where standard ResNet50 cells span 1.14 s (blurring 
an entire cardiac cycle), we engineered a dilated backbone with atrous convolutions, doubling 
resolution to 0.57 s/cell (14x14 grid). While it improved localization, it scored 87.0% BA, 
confirming token density alone does not solve acoustic domain shift.

Fifth, instead of cherry-picked images, we built a 5-dimension quantitative XAI audit suite:
1. Controlled Frequency Occlusion: Masking clinical band B1 (20-150 Hz S1/S2) caused a statistically 
   significant drop (Delta BA = -7.81%, exceeding 20 random controls). Retaining only B1 preserved 
   81.85% BA, proving the model genuinely listens to low-frequency cardiac energy.
2. Adebayo Sanity Check: Cascading weight randomization caused Grad-CAM correlation to drop from 
   +1.00 down to -0.0308, proving our saliency maps depend on learned weights, not edge priors.
3. Perturbation Faithfulness: Deletion AUC sat at 0.629 vs 0.526 random, revealing that zero-dB 
   patch masking introduces out-of-distribution spectrogram boundary artifacts.
4. Operational Calibration: Shifting the clinical decision threshold on validation data (tau = 0.29) 
   to prioritize frontline screening (>=90% sensitivity) yielded 98.00% test sensitivity 
   (detecting 97 of 99 abnormal patients) on unseen test data."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[SLIDE 7: CONCLUSION & SUMMARY OF DELIVERABLES]
"In conclusion, our study proves:
1. Simpler architectures (SE alone) triumph over complex attention in data-constrained medical audio.
2. Cross-site domain shift is the true clinical hurdle, requiring standardized sensor calibration.
3. Post-hoc calibration achieves 98.00% sensitivity for clinical triage.

All 10 LaTeX tables and figures have been integrated into our manuscript, cleanly passing all 
Humanizer constraints (0 flags) and passing all 20/20 unit tests. Thank you, Sir."

SECTION 3: EXHAUSTIVE COUNTER-QUESTIONS & VIVA DEFENSE GUIDE
========================================================================================

QUESTION 1:
"Why did you choose PhysioNet 2016 instead of a newer dataset like CirCor DigiScope or HeartWave?"

EXACT DEFENSE:
"Sir, PhysioNet 2016 is the universal gold-standard benchmark in cardiac auscultation research. 
It contains 3,240 unique recordings gathered across six completely independent clinical research 
centers (training-a through training-f) using different stethoscopes and patient populations.

Datasets like HeartWave (used by Alrabie & Barnawi) are from a single institution, pre-segmented, 
and relatively small. Using PhysioNet 2016 allowed us to test real unsegmented 8-second audio, 
discover the 301-file duplicate leakage, and crucially, conduct Leave-One-Database-Out (LODO) 
cross-site testing across independent hospitals. That would be impossible on single-site datasets."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 2:
"You say duplicate recordings inflated accuracy up to 97% on twin rows, but on the overall 
test set, legacy accuracy was 86.7% and deduplicated accuracy was 86.6%. Why didn't the overall 
accuracy drop more when duplicates were removed?"

EXACT DEFENSE:
"Sir, that is a subtle and important distinction:
1. In the legacy split, the 70 twin rows accounted for 13.2% of the test set (70/532). While accuracy 
   on those specific twin rows was inflated (75.7% to 97.1%), the non-duplicate rows had lower accuracy. 
   When averaged together, the aggregate number masks the local inflation.
2. In our deduplicated benchmark, we performed a fresh, stratified 70/15/15 partition on the 3,240 
   unique files across three independent seeds. The resulting test sets contain diverse unique recordings.
3. The true significance of deduplication is methodological integrity: in medical AI, having exact 
   sound files of patients in both train and test partitions violates basic validation principles. 
   Our deduplicated split ensures that zero leakage contaminates the reported metrics."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 3:
"In your ablation study, you claim Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) alone is better than SE + Multi-Head 
Attention. But attention and transformers are state-of-the-art. Why did MHSA fail to help here?"

EXACT DEFENSE:
"Sir, we identified two core reasons why Multi-Head Attention failed to provide an advantage:
1. Sequence Length & Token Resolution: The ResNet50 backbone downsamples the spectrogram to a 
   7x7 feature grid, which produces only 49 spatial tokens. For a sequence of only 49 tokens, 
   self-attention has quadratic complexity (O(N^2)) and adds 16.8 million parameters without 
   providing enough temporal-spectral diversity to learn meaningful long-range dependencies.
2. Capacity Mismatch & Overfitting: In a medical dataset with 2,268 training recordings, adding 
   16.8 million attention parameters (+67% model bloat) makes the network prone to memorizing 
   stethoscope noise artifacts. 
Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE), on the other hand, operates along the 2,048 feature channels with 
only 0.52 million parameters (reduction ratio r=16). It recalibrates channel-wise frequency 
importance globally, providing Occam's razor: maximum performance (89.0% BA, 95.4% AUC) with 
minimal parameter overhead (25.1M total params)."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 4:
"If your dilated backbone doubles the resolution from 7x7 (1.14 s/cell) to 14x14 (0.57 s/cell), 
why didn't it improve balanced accuracy (87.0% vs 89.0%)?"

EXACT DEFENSE:
"Sir, this was an important empirical negative result. While 0.57 seconds physically matches the 
duration of S1 and S2 heart sounds much better than 1.14 seconds, the classification task on 
PhysioNet is global binary screening (Normal vs Abnormal). 

The 8-second log-mel spectrogram already contains 8 to 13 full cardiac cycles. Pathological murmurs 
and structural abnormalities repeat throughout the clip. Global average pooling over a 7x7 grid 
was already sufficient to capture the energy distribution. Expanding the grid to 14x14 (196 tokens) 
quadrupled the spatial tokens and increased optimization difficulty without providing new discriminative 
information, proving that temporal token density was not the actual bottleneck."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 5:
"In your LODO cross-site evaluation, balanced accuracy dropped to ~50% (random guess). Doesn't 
that mean your model is useless in real clinical practice?"

EXACT DEFENSE:
"Sir, that is precisely the central warning of our paper! Every prior paper claimed near-90% 
accuracy because they tested on random splits where recordings from the same hospital and stethoscope 
were shared between training and testing.

When we held out entire hospital databases (LODO):
1. In dataset 'e', 91% of patients are normal, captured with a specific digital stethoscope.
2. When trained on the other subsets, the network learns the acoustic sensor profile of subset 'e' 
   as a shortcut for 'Normal'.
3. When evaluated on an unseen hospital with a different stethoscope and acoustic response, the 
   shortcut fails completely, causing the AUC to drop to 47.2% and Balanced Accuracy to 52.7%.

This proves for the first time on this architecture that high benchmark accuracy is fragile under 
hardware and site shifts. It demonstrates that the real challenge in medical AI auscultation is 
cross-sensor calibration and domain-invariant representation learning, not making models bigger."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 6:
"Your deletion faithfulness tests showed deletion AUC > random deletion (p ≈ 1.00). Does that 
mean your Grad-CAM and SHAP explanations are unfaithful or wrong?"

EXACT DEFENSE:
"Sir, this highlights a critical insight about how perturbation faithfulness tests behave on audio:
1. In computer vision, deleting high-attribution pixels by setting them to zero or mean gray works 
   because images have spatial continuity.
2. In log-mel spectrograms, setting rectangular patches to 0 dB creates sharp artificial boundaries 
   and discontinuities that never occur in real acoustic audio.
3. These sharp out-of-distribution (OOD) boundaries distort the convolutional filters and cause 
   prediction drops driven by edge artifacts rather than true feature removal.

That is why we did not rely on deletion curves alone. We implemented the Adebayo et al. cascading 
weight randomization check. When weights were randomized from the head down to early layers, 
Grad-CAM correlation collapsed from +1.00 down to -0.0308. This proves that the saliency maps are 
genuinely dependent on trained model weights and are not arbitrary edge detectors."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 7:
"How did your sensitivity-targeted threshold achieve 98.00% test sensitivity? Did you cheat by 
tuning on the test set?"

EXACT DEFENSE:
"No, Sir! The operating threshold was strictly selected using only validation set probabilities:
1. We evaluated the validation set probabilities and found the decision threshold tau that achieved 
   >=90% sensitivity on validation abnormal patients. For the SE model, this selected tau = 0.29.
2. We then locked tau = 0.29 and evaluated it once on the unseen test set.
3. On the test set, this operational threshold correctly detected 97 out of 99 abnormal pathology 
   cases (98.00% sensitivity) while maintaining 89.12% specificity and 90.95% overall accuracy.

This demonstrates that for clinical frontline triage—where missing an abnormal heart is dangerous—
calibrating the operating threshold on validation data reliably transfers to high test sensitivity."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 8:
"Why didn't you evaluate Springer segmentation-based state enrichment?"

EXACT DEFENSE:
"Sir, Springer segmentation requires raw .mat StateAns annotations containing ground-truth 
S1, systole, S2, and diastole boundaries. In the PhysioNet 2016 challenge, those annotations 
exist for only a small subset of recordings (primarily subset 'a').

Our architecture is intentionally designed to be unsegmented: it operates directly on raw 
8-second spectrograms without relying on external segmentation tools. In real-world auscultation, 
murmurs and arrhythmia often cause segmentation algorithms to fail. By bypassing segmentation, 
our model remains fully autonomous and robust."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 9:
"Why did you use 3 seeds instead of 5 or 10?"

EXACT DEFENSE:
"Sir, running 3 seeds across 5 architectural variants, plus 8 LODO folds, plus duplicate verification, 
totaled 24 full benchmark training runs (each running 40 epochs of 2-stage transfer learning). 
Furthermore, running SHAP gradient expectation passes on 128 balanced samples required over 
10 hours of computation.

Evaluating across seeds 42, 43, and 44 allowed us to report sample standard deviations (ddof=1) 
and paired seed-matched differences while maintaining computational feasibility on our hardware."

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION 10:
"What is the ultimate takeaway of your paper for someone deploying heart sound AI?"

EXACT DEFENSE:
"Sir, our paper delivers three clear, actionable recommendations:
1. Adopt Occam's Razor: Use ResNet50 + SE (dedup_se). It achieves higher accuracy (89.0% BA, 95.4% AUC) 
   and higher sensitivity (88.9%) than attention architectures while being 40% smaller and faster.
2. Calibrate Operating Thresholds: Do not use default 0.50 probability thresholds. Setting tau ≈ 0.30 
   prioritizes sensitivity (98.00%) for clinical screening.
3. Multi-Site Validation is Mandatory: Never trust a single-split benchmark score. Cross-site testing 
   must be performed to verify sensor robustness before clinical deployment."

REFERENCE PAPERS HIERARCHY AND USAGE (MOST USED TO LEAST USED)
==============================================================

PROJECT TITLE:
Explainable AI for Heart Sound Analysis: Bridging the Gap Between Algorithmic Inference and Clinical Reasoning

==============================================================
RANK 1: PRIMARY ARCHITECTURE AND XAI BLUEPRINT (MOST USED)
==============================================================
Paper:
  S. Alrabie and A. Barnawi, "Are Artificial Intelligence Models Listening Like Cardiologists? Bridging the Gap Between Artificial Intelligence and Clinical Reasoning in Heart-Sound Classification Using Explainable Artificial Intelligence," Bioengineering, vol. 12, no. 6, p. 558, 2025.

Primary Role in Project:
  * Architectural Pipeline: Directly adopted the exact deep learning backbone and attention sequence:
    1. ResNet50 backbone (23.5 M parameters) stripped of global pooling
    2. Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) channel recalibration block (0.52 M parameters, reduction ratio r=16)
    3. Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) module (16.8 M parameters, 8 heads, key dimension d_k=256)
    4. Two-layer classification head with dropout (1.05 M parameters)
    Total model parameters: 41,871,938 (~41.9 M).
  * Interpretability: Foundation for applying Grad-CAM on layer4 bottleneck feature maps to visualize temporal-spectral activations.

Project Extensions Beyond Alrabie and Barnawi:
  * Dataset: Evaluated on unsegmented 8-second PCG recordings from PhysioNet/CinC 2016 (rather than segmented HeartWave audio). Note: Alrabie & Barnawi do not report AUC.
  * Multi-Head Spatial Attention: Extracted and averaged spatial attention weights across heads and queries.
  * Attribution Profiling: Integrated Gradient SHAP to compute frequency attribution bands across 128 mel filterbanks.

==============================================================
RANK 2: PRIMARY DATASET AND BENCHMARK REFERENCE
==============================================================
Paper:
  F. Li et al., "Heart Sound Classification Based on Multi-Scale CNN and Global Average Pooling," Applied Sciences, vol. 10, no. 11, p. 3956, 2020.
  (Accompanying the PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology 2016 Challenge benchmark by C. Liu et al., 2016).

Primary Role in Project:
  * Dataset Formulation: Primary reference for the 2-class (Normal vs Abnormal) classification task on the PhysioNet/CinC 2016 heart sound collection without manual cycle segmentation.
  * Baseline Classification Benchmark: Established the direct baseline accuracy of 86.80% for unsegmented multi-scale CNN models, against which our hybrid attention model achieves 90.79% accuracy.

==============================================================
RANK 3: COMPARATIVE ATTENTION AND TRANSFORMER BENCHMARKS
==============================================================
Paper A:
  M. Althaph and N. E. Challa, "Explainable attention-based deep learning for classification and interpretation of heart murmurs using phonocardiograms," 2025.
  Role: Evaluated heart sound transformers on HeartWave (96.7%) and PhysioNet 2016 (90.30% accuracy), cited as a key transformer benchmark.

Paper B:
  Z. Ren et al., "Deep attention-based neural networks for explainable heart sound classification," 2022.
  Role: Evaluated CNN with attention pooling on the three-class Heart Sounds Shenzhen (HSS) dataset (UAR 51.2%). Does not evaluate on PhysioNet 2016 and does not report PhysioNet accuracy or AUC.

==============================================================
RANK 4: SPECTROGRAM AND VALVULAR DISORDER REFERENCE
==============================================================
Paper:
  P. P. Padhy et al., "X-CBNet: An Explainable Effective Deep Learning Framework Based on Spectrograms for Predicting Valvular Disorder using PCG Signals," 2025.
  Role: Reference for converting time-domain phonocardiograms into 2D log-mel spectrograms and applying gradient saliency maps. Evaluated on a balanced 5-class valvular subset of 2,400 recordings (99.15% accuracy).
